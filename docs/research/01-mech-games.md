## A. Gun Metal (Rage Software, 2002 Xbox / 2003 EU PC / 2005 NA PC "War Transformed" / 2014 Steam)

### A.1 Identity and disambiguation

- Developer: Rage Software (Liverpool, UK; shut down in 2003). Publisher: Majesco (North America, Xbox, August 2002); Rage Games (Europe). PC port published 2003 (EU) and re-released in North America as *Gun Metal: War Transformed* (2005). Steam release 2014 (app 267920), which ships the official English manual (`GunMetal_English.pdf`).
- **Do not confuse** with *Gunmetal* (one word, 1998, Mad Genius Software, MS-DOS/Windows). That is a first-person MechWarrior-style shooter with RPV loadouts; the GameFAQs "Gunmetal PC" guide by Dakhath99 covers *that* game, not Rage's.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_Metal_(video_game)), [MobyGames](https://www.mobygames.com/game/9741/gun-metal/), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/267920/Gun_Metal/), [Gunmetal (1998) Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunmetal_(video_game)), [Gunmetal disambiguation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunmetal_(disambiguation)).

### A.2 Premise

Human refugees from Earth's wars colonised the planet Helios. An Earth military force follows them to exterminate the settlers. The player pilots the prototype **Havoc Suit** - a 10-metre-tall humanoid mech that transforms "at the push of a button" into the **Havoc Jet**, an agile fighter. Marketing copy: "only this formidable battle machine has the potential to halt the invaders." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_Metal_(video_game)), [Codex Gamicus](https://gamicus.fandom.com/wiki/Gun_Metal), [Grokipedia summary](https://grokipedia.com/page/Gun_Metal_(video_game)))

### A.3 The transformation mechanic

What the sources establish:

- **Trigger**: one button, **Y** on the Xbox pad, in both directions (Suit -> Jet, Jet -> Suit). GameCritics: "you are free to change into the Havoc Jet at any time, with no limits or restrictions." OldPCGaming: "swapping from mech to jet can be done at any time and with the push of a button."
- **Time**: described as "instant" by Codex Gamicus and as "seamless" by Steam reviewers ("the overall feel is a seamless transformation from mech to plane and back again"). No source gives a frame/second count; **[inference]** it is a short (well under one second) blend animation with no lockout, since no reviewer mentions a vulnerability window.
- **What each form is for** (GameSpot, OldPCGaming, Steam guide): the robot "plods around at a very slow rate", so the loop is *transform to jet -> boost across the map -> transform back to robot to deal with ground-based foes*. Mech mode is "rather slow but packs heavy firepower your jet mode lacks". Jet mode is "required" because "many of the missions are timed" and the maps are large; it is also the tool for dogfights against enemy jets (the Jet's Vulcan cannons are "more effective against airborne enemies since they usually have less armor than ground enemies").
- **Handling**: the flight model is "extremely arcade" (OldPCGaming). Mech handling was criticised: "the maneuverability of your mech can be unresponsive at times, especially when you change the direction you are running" (Worthplaying). The mech has a jump (X button) which is explicitly recommended to un-stick yourself when standing on top of large ships (NeoY2J guide, mission 6).
- **Health regen is form-gated**: the Re-Energizer tower "slowly recharges your health when you stand near it **in mech mode**" (NeoY2J). This is a subtle, good design: the jet is the fast/fragile form and cannot heal; you must land and stand still to recover.
- **Weapon sets are form-gated and ammo pools are separate**: "You have separate stocks of missiles in the Havoc Suit & Havoc Jet modes" (official manual extract). The Suit has 12 weapons, the Jet has 12; one source says roughly four are shared between forms.

Sources: [GameCritics review](https://gamecritics.com/brad-gallaway/3290/), [GameSpot review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/gun-metal-review/1900-2878989/), [OldPCGaming](https://oldpcgaming.net/gun-metal-war-transformed/), [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2002/8/28/reviews/5456-xbox-review-gun-metal/), [NeoY2J GameFAQs guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/xbox/516236-gun-metal/faqs/18652), [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/267920/reviews?browsefilter=toprated), [Steam manual PDF](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/267920/manuals/GunMetal_English.pdf).

### A.4 Control scheme (Xbox, from the NeoY2J guide)

**Havoc Suit (mech)**

| Input | Function |
|---|---|
| Left stick | Move (walk/run, strafe) |
| Right stick | Turn / aim |
| L trigger | Launch auto-lock missiles |
| R trigger | Fire selected weapon |
| A | Cycle weapons |
| D-pad | Quick weapon select (four loadout slots) |
| X | Jump |
| Y | Transform to Jet |
| White / Black | Not assigned |

**Havoc Jet**

| Input | Function |
|---|---|
| Left stick | Turn / aim (pitch and bank) |
| Right stick | Boost (push forward) / Brake (pull back) |
| L trigger | Launch auto-lock missiles |
| R trigger | Fire selected weapon |
| A | Cycle weapons |
| D-pad | Quick weapon select |
| B | "Maneuver" (evasive move; the exact move is not named in extracts - likely a roll/tight turn **[inference]**) |
| Y | Transform to Suit |

Key design notes: **throttle on a stick axis, not a trigger**, so the jet has a continuous speed range plus brake; missiles are always on a dedicated trigger regardless of the four-slot loadout; weapon cycling is on a face button with D-pad direct-select as a faster alternative. The PC port maps this to mouse/keyboard, which Steam users call "slightly awkward" ("betrays its Xbox origins"). Source: [NeoY2J guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/xbox/516236-gun-metal/faqs/18652), [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/267920/reviews?browsefilter=toprated).

### A.5 Weapons (24 total; 12 per form; loadout of 4 per mission)

The manual/Armoury lets you "take up to four kinds of weapons for a mission", and "the more advanced armaments are unlocked as [you] progress" ([gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/games/gun-metal/z6bb2)). Unlocks are awarded per completed mission (Wikipedia: "as the player progresses through the game, additional weapons are unlocked"); the exact mission-to-weapon table was not recoverable from extracts. Weapons confirmed by name, with the properties the guides give:

**Havoc Suit (ground) weapons**
- **Machine Pistols** - default; fast, weak, **infinite ammo**. Starting kit for mission 1 alongside the Flak Gun and homing rockets.
- **Flak Gun** - short-range, high damage up close; the recommended tool for killing landed APCs in mission 3.
- **Homing rockets / Hellfire Missiles** - auto-lock missiles on the L trigger.
- **Assault Cannons** - ammo 50; "more power than the Flak Gun at close range, and you can fire these from anywhere"; slight bonus at close range.
- **Grenades** - ammo 30; lobbed, "very powerful, can take out most enemies with one grenade" but hard to use.
- **Torpedoes** - "hug the ground as they head toward their target" (ground-skimming guided projectile).
- **Disc Launcher** - "razor sharp disks will burn and rip through most targets but can also ricochet dangerously" (self-damage risk).
- **Trident** - launcher that fires **three homing missiles simultaneously** (gamepressure calls it "spectacular").
- **Ion Cannon** - heavy energy weapon.
- **Harpoon**, **Pulse Cannon**, **anti-air guns**, **demolition bombs** - named in Wikipedia/gamepressure; details not recovered.

**Havoc Jet (air) weapons**
- **Vulcan Cannons** - default; **infinite ammo**; "similar to the Suit's Machine Pistols"; best against lightly armoured aircraft.
- **Auto-lock missiles** (separate stock from the Suit's).
- **Mini Rockets**, **Rockets** - unguided.
- **Mavericks** - "big anti-plane rockets".
- **Phoenix** - ammo 40; "fires several shots in several directions, good for large groups" (spread/cluster).
- **Gauss Gun** - high-velocity single-shot.
- **Rapid Cannon**.
- **Napalm** and other **bombs** - ground attack from the air; "the bombs (particularly the Napalm) are difficult to aim" (Neoseeker user review).

Reviewer takes: KeenGamer - "the guns all feel pretty unique, but you get so many so fast that you find yourself struggling to distinguish between them in terms of function. A few feel like straight upgrades." GameSpot - "you earn lots of new weapons to choose from." Sources: [Codex Gamicus](https://gamicus.fandom.com/wiki/Gun_Metal), [NeoY2J](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/xbox/516236-gun-metal/faqs/18652), [gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/games/gun-metal/z6bb2), [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/other-reviews/gun-metal-war-transformed-review/), [Neoseeker user reviews](https://www.neoseeker.com/gunmetal/user_reviews/).

### A.6 Auto-lock missile system (the game's signature targeting)

From the official manual extract and NeoY2J: "When you launch a set of auto-lock missiles the counter is depleted by 1, and the number of auto-lock missiles at your disposal will increase as the game progresses." In play you hold fire while the reticule paints targets and "wait for 3-4 to be targeted before launching missiles, then use Vulcan Cannons for the rest." So: missiles are a *salvo* resource counted in salvos, not in individual rockets; a salvo multi-locks several enemies; the maximum simultaneous locks grows over the campaign. This is essentially a simplified Macross/ZOE multi-lock. Source: [manual via Steam CDN](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/267920/manuals/GunMetal_English.pdf), [NeoY2J](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/xbox/516236-gun-metal/faqs/18652).

### A.7 HUD and feedback

- **Targeting reticule**: turns **red** when an enemy is in range of the *currently selected weapon*; **blue** when the unit under it is friendly ("you should not fire"); displays **"NO TARGET"** when the selected weapon cannot hit what is under the cursor (e.g., an anti-air gun pointed at a tank). Range-awareness is thus baked into the reticule per weapon.
- **Radar disc**: colour-coded blips - **red** enemies, **blue** allies, **green** ammo pickups and Re-Energizers. Objectives are found by "finding the farms on the radar" (mission 1).
- **Missile counter**: number of remaining auto-lock salvos.
- **Shield/health**: the Havoc has an "electronic shield" plus a health bar; health recharges slowly near Re-Energizer towers (mech mode only).
- **Ammo pickups**: floating bullet icons restore **50% of max ammo** (two = full).
- Sources: [manual](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/267920/manuals/GunMetal_English.pdf), [NeoY2J](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/xbox/516236-gun-metal/faqs/18652), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_Metal_(video_game)).

### A.9 Enemy roster (Codex Gamicus + guides)

- **Infantry** (weakest, numerous, spawn from small **barracks** buildings - destroy the barracks to stop the flow): (1) trooper with small pulse rifle; (2) small robotic walker with continuous laser; (3) small walker with grenade launcher; (4) rare suicide bomber that detonates massively when destroyed.
- **Ground tanks**: common **missile tank**; stronger **heavy tank** with twin lasers.
- **Hover tanks**: flying, weaker than ground tanks but tougher than infantry; "attack in at least a squad of three."
- **Aircraft**: enemy **jets** (dogfight targets), **APC dropships** that land troops, plus bombers/gunships **[inference from mission descriptions]**.
- **Capital ships**: the **Leviathan** (walkable hull) and the endgame **mother ship**.
- **Allies**: allied helicopters/choppers and ground units exist but are weak ("allied choppers weakly try to put up an offense... you will likely take out more than the allies will"); friendly fire is possible, hence the blue reticule.
- Sources: [Codex Gamicus](https://gamicus.fandom.com/wiki/Gun_Metal), [NeoY2J](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/xbox/516236-gun-metal/faqs/18652), [XboxAddict review](https://www.xboxaddict.com/staff_review/4925.html).

### A.10 Reception: praise and criticism

- **Metacritic 63/100 (Xbox, "mixed")**, range 50-79. GameSpot **6.4**: "a middle-of-the-road game that makes only a few mistakes, but doesn't exactly reach for the stars"; "rent for a few days." Official Xbox Magazine **79**: "What it lacks in style or personality, Gun Metal more than makes up in gameplay and action." GameCritics (Brad Gallaway): "the game's strongest asset is the transformation gimmick, which is fun"; "as close as it gets to an Autobot until Robotech: Battlecry." Game Informer reviewed it in issue 114 (Oct 2002).
- **Praised**: the at-will transformation; hectic, arcade-style combat; tight controls (Steam); graphics "shockingly good" for 2002 (KeenGamer, 2020s retrospective); varied mission types; large maps; destructible terrain; weapon variety.
- **Criticised**: the mech is slow/plodding and sometimes unresponsive when changing direction; too many missions are defend/escort ("the problem with missions was that they consisted almost entirely of defending bases or playing escort to a group of ships" - XboxAddict; reviewers wanted more "one-on-one dogfights and full-on combat without worry of damaging friendly units"); weapons arrive too fast and blur together; bombs/napalm hard to aim; short (about 4 hours); little replay value beyond replaying missions with new loadouts; "lacks style or personality"; PC port has awkward mouse control and few graphics options.
- Sources: [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/gun-metal/), [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/gun-metal-review/1900-2878989/), [GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/brad-gallaway/3290/), [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/article/18420_gun-metal-war-transformed-review), [XboxAddict](https://www.xboxaddict.com/staff_review/4925.html), [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2002/8/28/reviews/5456-xbox-review-gun-metal/), [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/267920/reviews?browsefilter=toprated), [Classic Game Room video review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3RFHPbc1f0), [Hidden Gem? video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DEz5W8Jdx18).

### A.11 The "feel" summarised

Heavy, deliberate biped with a jump but no sustained hover; the jet is a pure arcade plane with stick-axis throttle and brake, no stall model mentioned; transformation is free and instantaneous, which is what makes the game work - the mech's slowness is not a problem because the jet is one button away, and the jet's lack of firepower/healing is not a problem because the mech is one button away. The tension in the design is entirely *which form am I in right now*, not *can I afford to transform*.

### B.2 What Granvir is

Full release September 2025 with native Linux support; *Granvir: Zero Front* is a free standalone prologue ("first wave of resistance fighters battling the invading Millennium Company") whose progress and an exclusive part carry into the full game. ### B.3 Mechanics

- **Assembly**: ~300 parts. Every Granvir must have a **Torso, Legs and Reactor**; everything else (arms, shoulders, back, weapons) is optional. Dual wielding; melee weapons such as the *rocket spear* and *ultra claymore*.
- **Heat as the action economy**: "Performing dashes, jumps, or special actions will gradually fill up your Granvir's Heat gauge, eventually disabling these actions until it's sufficiently cooled down. Better Reactors tend to have better Heat capacity." The Beginner Tips guide says the two priority upgrades are Legs and Reactor (aim for Tier 2). "With a fast cooling reactor, you have the ability to just keep dodging all fight long."
- **Leg classes define movement**: bipedal legs "trade the rapid strafing of spider-tank-esque quadrupedal legs for faster dashing and higher forward momentum"; **Equine** legs (Feb 2026 update) push that further; **rocket-powered skates** give "ludicrous top speed"; some parts have a quadruped transformation mode (the *Daumenfittich* got +100 cooling rate in its quadruped mode). A physics rule: "all legs are now capable of **sliding when they exceed their top speed**, and while sliding, control is greatly diminished until the Granvir slows below its top speed."
- **Targeting**: crosshair-aimed third-person shooting; the first post-launch update added **lock-on missiles** on three parts; there are **auto-targeting toggle weapons** (torso machine guns) with a crosshair notification for on/off. Criticism: some "lock-on" guns only fire at the crosshair rather than tracking the locked target; the strafe/dodge felt too short to be useful to one reviewer.
- **Campaign layer**: a branching overworld where you "manage time, resources and your route to each objective", zones change which services are available, rest areas have a **squad depot** for sharing parts in co-op, and parts drop from supply drops and enemy caches. Reviewers compare the structure favourably to Hades/Dead Cells because the map is "a living, branching world".
- Sources: [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2743460/Granvir/), [Beginner Tips guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3584896991), [Part Comparisons guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3707612751), [Dev Update #5: Legs](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2743460/view/4139442565106311091), [Update 0.11.0](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2743460/view/4338740162774659415), [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2743460/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated), [GameReed](https://gamereed.com/granvir).

## C. Vital Shell - identification and mechanics

**Found - high confidence.** *Vital Shell* is a Steam game (app 3741860) by solo developer **MarvinWizard**, released **7 January 2026**; top-down arena roguelike shooter with a deliberate PS1/PSX look and an ambient jungle soundtrack. Steam rating Overwhelmingly Positive (97% of ~1,700 reviews). Tagline used by press: "Armored Core meets Vampire Survivors."

Mechanics:
- **Run structure**: pick a Shell (mech) and a stage; survive **20 waves of 30 seconds each** (19 normal + 1 boss). Boss = a *corrupted version of another Shell*; beating it **unlocks that Shell and the next stage**. Five stages, five Shells.
- **Shells (fantasy archetypes as mechs)**:
  - **Koenig** (knight): broadsword; boost is a shoulder-check that damages enemies, scaling with Armour and Block.
  - **Mirage** (wizard): "magic bazooka" whose element changes every few seconds.
  - **Bloodfang** (four-armed rocket brute): *Rocket Storm* ability (achievement for 20 rockets in one activation).
  - **Sidewinder** (rogue/summoner): slow-charging railgun plus two mini-Shell pets; boost pulls pets to you, damaging things in their path.
  - **Meteor** (reaper): scythe with passive lifesteal; dash creates a **gravity well** that pulls enemies and even bosses off their patterns.
- **Build-crafting**: after each wave choose a stat boost, new weapon, or weapon mod. Four weapon slots per chassis; each weapon has **four gem sockets** (lifesteal, crit rate, attack speed, etc.). **Devotions** are permanent meta-progression stat lines - only one active at a time, levelled to a max of 10 by finishing runs. Dodge upgrades lower dash cooldown (achievement for 150 boosts in one run). Steam Workshop support.
- Sources: [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3741860/Vital_Shell/), [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/VitalShell), [Time Extension](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2026/01/mech-shooter-vital-shell-absolutely-nails-that-ps1-era-vibe), [Kay Talks Games](https://kaytalksgames.wordpress.com/2026/02/06/vital-shell-is-vampire-survivors-with-mechs/), [CrystalPixie on Medium](https://medium.com/@magpie.nexus.nest/vital-shell-gods-gift-for-freaks-like-me-5abb9b80fec7), [Mech Game Wiki](https://mech-game.fandom.com/wiki/Vital_Shell), [achievements](https://steamhunters.com/apps/3741860/achievements).

## D. Cousins: transferable mechanics

### D.1 Zone of the Enders: The 2nd Runner (Konami, 2003)
- **Distance-contextual attack button**: one button (Square) is a melee dash-slash at close range and a homing-laser volley at long range; the game decides by distance to the locked target. Lock-on is L2; dash is R2 (hold to keep dashing, tap repeatedly for bursts).
- **Multi-lock by holding**: while dashing, *hold* Square - green circles paint every enemy in view - *release* to fire homing lasers at all of them; anything between you and the target intercepts the shot.
- **Grab/throw** as default sub-weapon, sub-weapons on the D-pad (Decoy, Javelin, Sniper, Phalanx, Geyser, Comet, Halberd, Gauntlet, Bounder, Mummy, Vector Cannon, Homing Missile, Floating Mine, Zero Shift). *Zero Shift* teleports you to the locked target; *Vector Cannon* roots you for a devastating charged shot.
- Transferable: (1) hold-to-paint / release-to-fire multi-lock; (2) one attack button whose result depends on range; (3) a teleport-to-target burst move.
- Sources: [arcor guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps2/561586-zone-of-the-enders-the-2nd-runner/faqs/21902), [Iluna guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps2/561586-zone-of-the-enders-the-2nd-runner/faqs/21844), [Majin_Cloud subweapon FAQ](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps2/561586-zone-of-the-enders-the-2nd-runner/faqs/22267), [Konami ZOE HD system page](https://www.konami.com/games/zoe_hd/en_us/gamesystem_zoe.html).

### D.2 Armored Core VI: Fires of Rubicon (FromSoftware, 2023)
- **Assault Boost**: overcharged boosters for "vastly increased speed at the cost of higher energy drain"; movement is "largely locked into a straight line, turning is slow and heavily restricted" until you cancel; ends with a melee kick option.
- **ACS / stagger**: every hit adds **Impact** to a bar; when it fills the target is staggered - a **fixed ~2-second** stun during which Direct Hit damage is amplified, and the bar hard-resets about 3.5 s after the stagger. The threshold is the sum of head/core/legs **Attitude Stability**; **Attitude Recovery** sets how fast accumulated impact decays. Shotguns, missiles, grenades, bazookas have high Impact; melee has high Direct Hit.
- **Soft lock vs hard lock (Target Assist)**: soft lock auto-highlights any enemy in view within FCS range and lets weapons track it; R3 hard-locks, centring the camera on one target with a white ring. Hard lock for duels; soft lock when surrounded. The **FCS part** governs tracking range/speed and multi-lock efficiency.
- Transferable: (1) a straight-line, high-drain "assault" dash with a kick finisher; (2) impact/stagger meter with a fixed stun window; (3) two-tier lock-on.
- Sources: [Assault Boost wiki](https://armoredcore.fandom.com/wiki/Assault_Boost), [Prima stagger guide](https://primagames.com/tips/armored-core-6-how-to-make-the-most-of-enemy-acs-stagger-in-ac6), [VGKAMI attitude stability](https://vgkami.com/armored-core-6-attitude-stability-recovery-explained/), [Steam thread on 2 s stagger](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1888160/discussions/0/3827551893137303922), [Game8 lock-on](https://game8.co/games/armored-core-6/archives/425274), [Sportskeeda Assault Boost](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/how-use-assault-boost-armored-core-6).

### D.3 Daemon X Machina (Marvelous, 2019)
- **Two movement fuels**: *Femto* = flight fuel (sustained hover + "Fusion" specials; slow recharge; replenished by flying through drops from destroyed enemies or map crystals); *Stamina* = boost/dodge/sprint fuel, fast recharge. Hovering just off the ground costs less than half the stamina of running, so the intended locomotion is a low skim.
- **Femto Shifts** (power / shield / flight): each boosts one stat while lowering another; *Wing Shift* grants unlimited stamina while the Femto meter lasts.
- Automatic lock-on with range increased by gear.
- Transferable: (1) separate short-recharge dodge fuel and long-recharge flight fuel; (2) a "shift" stance that trades stats; (3) refuel-by-flying-through-drops.
- Sources: [Gamepur Femto](https://www.gamepur.com/guides/daemon-x-machina-how-the-femto-system-works), [Zeph's Den tips](https://zephsden.wordpress.com/2023/01/09/daemon-x-machina-starting-tips/), [Zelda Universe review](https://zeldauniverse.net/features/review-daemon-x-machina-offers-a-lot-but-doesnt-always-deliver/).

### D.4 Macross / Robotech games (Fighter - Gerwalk - Battroid)
- **Macross Ace Frontier / Ultimate Frontier (PSP, Artdink)**: the **right analog nub is the transform control** - push up = Fighter, sideways = Gerwalk, down = Battroid. X = thrusters (speed in Fighter; altitude gain in Gerwalk/Battroid). D-pad left/right = barrel roll in Fighter, side-step dodge in other modes; D-pad down = drop the legs in Fighter for a hard brake; braking hard in Fighter auto-drops you into Gerwalk. At full engine output in Fighter you outrun missiles. Special "Fight combo" auto-transforms to Battroid/Gerwalk, dashes to melee range and combos; melee kills feed the post-mission rank.
- **Macross 30 (PS3)**: D-pad picks mode; lock is *held* on L1; in Battroid and Gerwalk "locks tend to slip off enemies, requiring you to actively aim" - i.e., the humanoid forms are manual-aim precision modes, the fighter is the auto-track mode. Two control types, one with a manually movable lock cursor.
- **Robotech: Battlecry (Vicious Cycle, 2002)**: transform on the D-pad (criticised for forcing thumb off the stick). Fighter: slowest turning but the most powerful missiles, hold-fire lasers; Guardian (Gerwalk): light missiles, can hover, pick up/drop items; Battloid: can shoot down incoming missiles, gun-pod gatling. Reviewer-described loop: "turbo-boost in with fighter mode, switch to Battroid and unload the Gatling." Criticised for sluggish controls and "near-total absence of a sense of speed."
- Transferable: (1) transform bound to a direction, not a cycle button; (2) each mode owns a distinct weapon *and* a distinct defensive verb (roll vs side-step vs shoot-down-missiles); (3) hard brake in fighter automatically drops one mode.
- Sources: [Ace Frontier controls thread](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/948812-macross-ace-frontier/45862921), [Ace Frontier translation guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/psp/948812-macross-ace-frontier/faqs/54833), [Ultimate Frontier guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/psp/961063-macross-ultimate-frontier/faqs/60727), [Macross 30 Bordersdown review](https://bordersdown.net/forum/gaming/first-play/60839-macross-30-ps3-review), [Macross 30 guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps3/687898-macross-30-ginga-o-tsunagu-utagoe/faqs/69704), [Skyward FM on Macross flight controls](https://www.skywardfm.com/post/real-flight-controls-in-macross-games), [Battlecry GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/robotech-battlecry-review/1900-2881427/), [Battlecry GameFAQs guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/xbox/543639-robotech-battlecry/faqs/19483), [Mecha Talk thread](https://www.mechatalk.net/viewtopic.php?t=13171).

### D.5 MechWarrior 4 / Starsiege (sim lineage)
- MW4: heat gauge and coolant, **torso twist** independent of leg heading (some mechs 360 deg), weapon groups with chain/alpha fire, in-range indicator, jump jets.
- Starsiege (Dynamix 1999) **removed torso twist** and instead gave a **free-floating reticle** moved by the mouse inside the view; HERCs have rechargeable **shields** with adjustable facing focus (which reviewers found "too much to concentrate on" in a fight); ~30 highly configurable vehicles.
- Gunvalkyrie (Xbox 2002): boost by clicking the stick, jump-boost on the left trigger, chain them to stay airborne; the Boost Gauge reaching 25 enters a "Mobius" state; mid-air boost combos refill fuel. Gungriffon Blaze: one pilot with multi-target lock, another with splash-aimed weapons, giving two distinct play feels.
- Transferable: (1) a floating reticle inside a wider view cone instead of full torso twist (cheap to implement in a browser, avoids camera fights); (2) a heat cap as the limit on burst actions; (3) a boost-chain meter that rewards staying airborne.
- Sources: [MW4 quick reference](https://www.scribd.com/document/68139055/MechWarrior-4-Vengeance-Quick-Reference-Card), [MWO forum on reticle/torso](https://mwomercs.com/forums/topic/204-unlock-reticle-aiming-from-torso-twist/), [Starsiege Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starsiege), [Starsiege Usenet review](https://groups.google.com/g/comp.sys.ibm.pc.games.action/c/1sce2SusUpc), [Gunvalkyrie guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/xbox/475252-gunvalkyrie/faqs/16419), [Gungriffon Mecha Damashii](https://www.mechadamashii.com/features/features-gungriffon-the-forgotten-conflict/).

## E. Design takeaways for a browser Gundam game with transform

The user's game is a browser Gundam title with a transforming suit (think Zeta/Waverider, or ReZEL/Methuss/Asshimar). Below, "MS" = mobile-suit form, "WR" = waverider/flight form. Numbers are starting proposals to tune, chosen to sit inside the ranges the sourced games use.

### E.1 Transformation
1. **One key, both directions, no cost, no cooldown** (Gun Metal's core lesson; GameCritics called it the game's strongest asset). Bind to a single key (e.g., `F` / gamepad Y). Do *not* put it on a D-pad or a stick-direction gesture - Battlecry was dinged for thumb travel.
2. **Blend time 0.25-0.35 s**, during which you are **invulnerable to collision damage but can still be shot**, and the previous form's momentum carries over (MS->WR converts current velocity into forward WR speed; WR->MS keeps the velocity vector and applies MS drag). This preserves "seamless" while giving a tiny animation read.
3. **Auto-transform edge cases** (Macross Ace Frontier): pull the brake fully in WR for >0.4 s -> automatically drops to MS (hover). Touch ground in WR below 20% throttle -> auto MS. Removes crash frustration in a browser where input reliability varies.
4. **Momentum is the point**: WR top speed ~3x MS run speed (Gun Metal: "plodding" vs "boosting to where you need to be"). Example: MS run 12 m/s, MS boost dash 30 m/s for 0.5 s, WR cruise 40 m/s, WR afterburner 70 m/s.

### E.2 Form differentiation (each form owns a weapon set, a defensive verb, and a regen rule)
5. **Weapon sets are per-form with separate ammo pools** (Gun Metal manual). Loadout screen: pick **4 slots total**, e.g., 2 MS + 2 WR, plus a form-agnostic missile salvo counter. MS gets beam rifle / beam saber / bazooka / shield; WR gets nose vulcan (infinite), forward missiles, a strafing bomb, and a fixed mega-particle/beam cannon that fires along the flight vector.
6. **Defensive verbs differ** (Macross/Battlecry): MS = shield block (front 90 deg, absorbs 100% of first 300 damage then breaks for 6 s) and side-step; WR = barrel roll (0.6 s, breaks missile lock, i-frames vs projectiles only) and outrun missiles above 60 m/s.
7. **Healing/repair only in MS** and only while stationary near a friendly "re-energizer" (Gun Metal): 2% HP per second within 25 m of a base beacon, form-gated. Ammo crates restore 50% of max per pickup (Gun Metal's number).
8. **Melee is MS-only and the highest DPS** (Macross rank bonus, ZOE dash-slash): beam saber 3-hit combo 3 x 120 damage; dash-slash if the attack key is pressed while boosting toward a target inside 15 m.

### E.3 Targeting and lock-on
9. **Two-tier lock** (AC6): soft lock automatically highlights the nearest enemy inside a 30 deg cone within 400 m; a tap of `Tab`/R3 hard-locks (camera centres, ring turns white); hard lock can be broken only by the target leaving 600 m or dying.
10. **Missile salvos count as one resource** (Gun Metal): the missile counter is *salvos*, e.g., 6 per mission; holding the missile key paints up to N targets (N = 2 at mission 1 growing to 6 by the end, exactly Gun Metal's "increases as the game progresses"); release to fire (ZOE hold/release). Paint time 0.35 s per target.
11. **Reticle semantics** (Gun Metal): red = in range of the *selected* weapon; blue = friendly (fire disabled or heavily penalised); grey "NO TARGET" text when the selected weapon cannot hit this target class (e.g., bombs vs aircraft). This single indicator solves the friendly-fire complaints reviewers had.
12. **Lock slips in MS at long range, sticks in WR** (Macross 30): in MS the soft-lock cone is 30 deg and the reticle drifts, rewarding manual aim; in WR the cone is 60 deg and locks persist, because you cannot realistically aim a plane and fly it with a keyboard.

### E.4 Movement feel
13. **MS boost/thruster fuel** modelled as DxM's two meters: *Thruster* (dodge/dash; 100 units, dash costs 30, recharges 40/s after a 0.5 s delay) and *Flight* (hover/climb; 100 units, drains 15/s while airborne, recharges 10/s only when landed). Hovering 2 m above ground costs 40% of full flight drain, so the intended MS locomotion is a low skim, not walking.
14. **Heat as the limiter for special actions** (Granvir): beam cannon, assault dash, and repeated dashes add heat; at 100 heat these actions lock out until heat falls below 60; cooling 20/s. Better "reactor" upgrades raise cap/cooling.
15. **Assault Boost analogue in WR** (AC6): afterburner locks turning to 25% authority, drains flight fuel 3x, and ends in a **ram/kick** if you collide with an enemy (impact 400, self-stagger immune). Give it a 0.3 s wind-up so it is readable.
16. **Over-speed slide** (Granvir): if MS exceeds run top speed (after WR->MS transformation), control authority drops to 30% until it decelerates below top speed - turns the transform into a drift you must plan for.

### E.5 Stagger/impact
17. **Impact meter on every enemy** (AC6): bazooka 80, missiles 40 each, vulcan 3/round, saber 60/hit; enemy stability 300-1500 by class; stagger = **2.0 s** fixed stun, damage x1.5, bar resets 3.5 s after. Player also has stability (800 base) - being staggered mid-WR forces an MS drop.

### E.6 Mission and level structure (learn from Gun Metal's reviews)
18. **Keep Gun Metal's 14-mission scale and 4-hour length**, but cap defend/escort at **40%** of missions; reviewers' top complaint was "almost entirely defending bases or playing escort." Alternate: assault, dogfight-only, boarding a capital ship (Leviathan), minesweeping, infiltration.
19. **Each mission introduces exactly one thing**: a new enemy class (infantry from barracks -> missile tank -> heavy laser tank -> hover-tank trio -> APC drops -> capital ship -> mother ship), or one new weapon, never both - KeenGamer's "too many guns too fast" complaint.
20. **Timed traversal objectives** every third mission force the WR form (Gun Metal: "many missions are timed"); pure-dogfight missions force WR; boarding missions force MS.
21. **Escort AI must be strong enough to matter**: give escorts 2x the HP the player expects and make allies shoot down 30% of incoming, so the player is a force multiplier, not a babysitter (the "allied choppers weakly try" complaint).
22. **Destructible barracks/spawners** (Gun Metal infantry): every infantry stream has a visible spawner with 500 HP; killing it stops the stream. Cheap, readable, satisfying.

### E.7 HUD (browser-friendly)
23. **Radar disc** bottom-left, 200 m radius, red enemies / blue allies / green pickups & beacons / yellow objectives; a thin outer ring shows off-radar objective bearing.
24. **Form badge** with the transform key printed on it, and the *other* form's two weapons ghosted beneath the current four, so the player always knows what a transform will give them.
25. **Salvo counter** (missiles) separate from the per-weapon ammo, plus heat and flight-fuel bars stacked under the reticle so eyes never leave the crosshair.

### E.8 Meta (from Granvir / Vital Shell)
26. **Post-mission loadout unlock plus one "devotion"-style permanent stat line** (Vital Shell): one active passive, levelled by finishing missions, max 10 - gives replay value that Gun Metal lacked ("trying old missions in new ways is really the only replay value").
27. **Wave timer readability** (Vital Shell's 20 x 30 s): for defend missions, show wave count and a 30 s timer so "defend" has a visible finish line.
